## Subtopics nobody covers well — our unique sections

1. **INA 101(a)(13)(C) as the organising idea.** Every other page starts from "how long can I be away". The statute starts from a different question — whether you are an applicant for admission at all — and the six conditions in (C) are what actually determine whether a trip is routine or a legal event. Writing the page from the statute down, rather than from the day count up, is the structural differentiator.
2. **N-470, and its one-year filing cliff.** An Indian family posted back to India for a two-year assignment who want to keep the naturalisation clock have exactly one instrument, and it expires as an option the day the absence hits twelve months. It appears on no competitor page and nowhere on this site.
3. **The two consequences of a 6–12 month trip, separated.** Card validity and continuous residence are different regimes with different thresholds, and conflating them is the most common error in the organic set.
4. **ICE detention, from the primary source.** ICE's own published average daily population: FY2019 50,165 → FY2021 19,254 → FY2024 37,722 → FY2025 48,404 → FY2026 64,508 (October 2025 – July 2026). The honest reading — that the population has risen sharply while the specific risk to a returning LPR with clean history remains governed by 101(a)(13)(C) — is more useful than either reassurance or alarm, and it is what the data supports.
5. **A maintainable tracker.** `data/ice-detention-tracker.json`: dated entries, each with a source URL and a category, policy changes and reported case *patterns* only. No private individuals. The owner adds a row; the page and its "last updated" line follow.
6. **Interactive: trip risk checker.** Departure date, planned return, reentry permit held, prior long trips, pending applications, any arrests or charges (yes/no, nothing stored) → a risk tier, the documents to carry, and the point at which the answer is "talk to a lawyer before you book". Browser-only. No competitor has a tool on this topic.
7. **The airport scenario table** — what to do and what not to do, including the three things that turn a routine secondary referral into an abandonment finding.

## Editorial constraints applied

- Calm and factual. No fear language, no implied prediction of what will happen to any individual reader.
- The tracker records policy changes and aggregate patterns. Private individuals are not named.
- Nothing in the know-your-rights section is sourced to advocacy material; every line traces to a statute, a regulation, or an official agency page.
- The word "caveat" does not appear.
