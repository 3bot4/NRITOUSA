## Subtopics nobody covers well — our unique sections

1. **O-1 as the escape from the H-1B cap *and* the H-1B clock.** 8 CFR 214.2(o)(13) says an approved PERM or a filed preference petition is not a basis for denying an O-1 petition, an extension or admission. Combined with uncapped 1-year extensions, an O-1 holder in the India EB queue has no six-year wall — the thing that defines H-1B life for an Indian applicant. No competitor connects (o)(13) to the India backlog.
2. **The admissions data, with India in it.** FY2024: 122,160 O-1 admissions, of which India 5,080 (4.2%) against Canada 20,320 and Mexico 6,170 — while India takes 497,460 of 890,310 H-1B admissions (55.9%). The O-1 is drastically under-used by Indian nationals relative to every other route. That is the page's thesis and it is a fact, not an opinion.
3. **Which of the eight criteria an Indian tech career actually produces** — and which it structurally does not. Patents and "original contributions of major significance" are reachable from industry work; "membership in associations requiring outstanding achievements" and "a panel or judge of the work of others" are reachable cheaply and are routinely left on the table.
4. **The three standards, named.** Science/education/business/athletics = "one of the small percentage who have arisen to the very top". Arts = "distinction". Motion picture/TV = "extraordinary achievement". Competitors print two.
5. **The fee stack as a stack**, with the one-beneficiary-per-O-1-petition rule and the small-employer halving — which matters because the Indian founder audience for this page *is* a small employer.
6. **Stamping in India on an O-1**, and that O-1 is not interview-waiver eligible under the rules effective 1 October 2025 — links to `/visa-interview-waiver`.
7. **Interactive: O-1A criteria self-check.** Tick the evidence you hold per criterion; it reports how many of the eight are plausibly met, which are closest to reachable, and what the final-merits determination still means. Browser-only, nothing stored. No competitor has a tool.
